METIS - Advanced Research Idea Generation
Proposal inspired by "The Alien Space of Science" (arXiv 2603.01092v3, 21 Oct 2026)
Pinned source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01092v3
Prepared 2026-10-30. Status: proposal only. Not checked against current Metis code.
No current interfaces, no implementation authority, no validated results claimed.
Stays inside Metis: nothing here depends on ASOS.


PROPOSED NAME

Heuresis (proposed only, not accepted) - Greek heuresis, "discovery / a
finding". Fits the Metis convention of the exact Greek word for what the
component does (LTHCM remembers, dianoia thinks; heuresis would discover).


WHAT THE PAPER OFFERS (v3, author-reported, not reproduced)

The paper's useful idea is architectural: separate the search for concept
combinations from the generation of prose about them.

- An LLM distils papers into short method statements; HDBSCAN clusters them
  into reusable "idea atoms" (their corpus: 16,068 LLM papers, 273 atoms,
  about 3.35M candidate triples).
- Coherence scorer: a small transformer (about 43M params) judging whether
  atoms plausibly co-occur in one work.
- Availability scorer: a two-encoder contrastive model over about 39.8k
  author profiles, judging whether any existing community is positioned to
  produce the combination. Their hypothesis: high coherence + low
  availability marks under-explored candidates. That is a search hypothesis,
  not evidence those candidates are good science.
- Scores are normalised (z-scores) and blended; ranking about 3.35M triples
  reportedly took under 15 minutes with no LLM call per candidate (corpus
  prep, distillation and training are separate, unpriced costs). An LLM
  writes up only the few selected candidates.
- Forward-in-time test: everything built from pre-2025 data (191 atoms from
  8,148 papers), tested against 2025 papers. Top 10,000 of about 1.14M
  candidates recover 762 of 2,424 real 2025 combinations (35.9x chance); on
  strictly novel combinations coherence alone finds 29 (2.35x) and adding
  unavailability finds 40 (3.24x) while recovering fewer total. One future
  year only.
- Diversity: effective distinct ideas about 225 for their method vs 57/97 for
  independent Claude/Gemini prompting; batch prompting reaches 198/183 and
  random 250. Diversity is not quality.
- Quality evidence is thin: human study of 10 ideas per method, their method
  ties Claude at 4.03; automated research test judged by GPT-5.5 with 68%
  expert agreement and a wide interval. Temporal recovery and diversity do
  not measure scientific quality or feasibility.

PROPOSED FIT FOR METIS

Heuresis would be a research-idea module beside the existing stack. The
mappings below are to each component's recorded purpose, not to verified
current behaviour; no current interfaces are claimed, and every storage or
write it needs is proposed and user-approved, not assumed.

1. Corpus and atoms - stoma and LTHCM (proposed roles)
   - stoma's recorded purpose is ingestion (PDFs, docs, repos, audio, chat
     exports). Heuresis would propose a distillation mode alongside that
     role: an LLM turns ingested papers into conceptual units as a batch job.
   - LTHCM's recorded purpose is the memory substrate. Heuresis would propose
     storing atoms and their provenance (atom -> units -> source documents)
     there as new records, with the user's approval; no modification to LTHCM
     itself is assumed.
   - Units that do not cluster are proposed to be kept rather than dropped:
     they are the pool from which genuinely new concepts could later emerge.

2. Combination exploration - batch scoring, optionally dianoia later
   - Scoring and ranking candidate atom combinations is a bounded batch job:
     candidate triples in, scored candidates out. It does not need dianoia's
     walk loop. A walk action for guided exploration is a later option only,
     and would have to earn its place in dokime.
   - The point for Metis: candidates come from a scored search over what is
     actually in the corpus, with provenance - not from a model
     free-associating "ideas" from its weights.

3. Selection - krisis (proposed, calibrated)
   - krisis' recorded purpose is deterministic selection with abstain.
     Heuresis would propose abstain thresholds over the normalised scores,
     calibrated on held-out data. This is a design proposal to be tuned and
     measured, not a guaranteed clean mapping: if calibration shows the
     thresholds do not separate useful candidates, the answer is to abstain
     or redesign, not to lower the bar silently. Returning "nothing worth
     proposing from this corpus" is a valid outcome.
   - Each selected candidate record carries: the research question; the atom
     IDs and source unit IDs as provenance; the normalised coherence and
     community-availability scores kept separate; a novelty-search status
     (not yet checked / checked against the literature with result) - low
     community availability alone does not establish novelty; a proposed
     falsifiable test and its failure criterion; and an estimated budget for
     investigating it, marked unknown until measured.

4. Scorers - paideia (requires a verified training backend)
   - Both scorers are small trainable models; paideia is their proposed home.
   - Historical caution, not a current verification: a past review (@
     aacb3e58) found paideia's QLoRA runner reported COMPLETED without
     actually training. Whether that still holds is unchecked. Requirement
     for Heuresis: any training backend must be freshly verified to really
     train before scorer training is attempted. No hardware assumptions;
     local training and ranking cost is unmeasured.

5. Write-up and eval - hestia and dokime
   - Write-up of selected candidates would be served through hestia, with the
     model choice left open and measured in dokime rather than assumed.
     Write-up is the only LLM-per-candidate step.
   - dokime gets two concrete, separate evals:
     a. Ranking eval (the paper's forward-in-time test, directly reusable):
        split the corpus by date and freeze everything (atomization, unit
        extraction, clustering, both scorers) to the earlier slice, with the
        later slice mapped onto that frozen vocabulary. Tune beta and
        calibration on a validation cut of the earlier slice only; the final
        future test runs once, untouched by tuning. Measure recovery of later
        real combinations vs chance, plus diversity of the selected set. This
        tests ranking behaviour, not idea quality.
     b. Proposal-quality eval: blind human scoring of written-up candidates
        on quality, feasibility and novelty against baselines (independent
        LLM prompting, batch prompting, random). This is the test the paper
        itself barely runs, and it matters more than (a) before trusting
        output.

6. Guardrails and interface - phylax, kybernetes, prosopon
   - Heuresis proposes; phylax would gate anything beyond proposal. Results
     and their scores/provenance would be proposed for the prosopon
     dashboard. All orchestration through kybernetes is proposed wiring, not
     an existing path.


WHY THIS COULD BE WORTH BORROWING FOR METIS

- It would give Metis a generative research capability grounded in its own
  corpus: every proposal traceable to atoms, units and sources.
- It reinforces the stack's split of duties instead of adding a monolithic
  "idea prompt": search over memory, calibrated selection, separate write-up,
  separate measurement.
- The diversity result is the failure mode to design against: independent LLM
  prompting concentrates on a few obvious combinations. Heuresis would exist
  to beat that, and dokime would measure whether it does.
- The main scientific caution: the method recombines concepts already in its
  vocabulary. It does not demonstrate invention of genuinely new concepts,
  and all its evidence comes from LLM research papers.


BOUNDARIES

- Spec-level proposal. The umbrella repo was not reviewed for this; no
  current module is claimed to expose the interfaces described above, and
  recorded component purposes are not verified current behaviour.
- Paper results are author-reported; quality evidence is thin (10 ideas per
  method, LLM judges). Promising, not proven.
- The availability scorer assumes author-publication histories; on a private
  corpus the analogue (source/project/topic groupings) is a new variant
  needing its own validation.
- Local cost (distillation volume, scorer training, ranking, write-up) is
  unmeasured; no hardware fit is claimed.
- Nothing here mixes with ASOS. If a shared corpus or shared scorers ever
  make sense, that is a separate, explicit decision.


SUGGESTED VALIDATION PATH (bounded first)

1. Smoke test on a small bounded corpus (a few hundred papers): distillation,
   clustering, both scorers (or a stand-in for availability), ranking. Goal:
   real cost numbers and sane atoms. Full-scale reproduction of the paper's
   pipeline is optional and cost-gated on user approval, not a prerequisite.
2. dokime ranking eval: forward-in-time split on that corpus, recovery vs
   chance and diversity coverage. Tests ranking, not quality.
3. dokime proposal-quality eval: blind human scoring of written-up proposals
   (quality, feasibility, novelty) against baselines, before Heuresis output
   is treated as more than an experiment.
