METIS: EXECUTABLE EVALUATION CASES AND BOUNDARY-AWARE TASK SELECTION
Evolve on Graph takeaways | 28 October 2026

Status: two proposed Metis adaptations, not implemented features or authority to
change code, train models, download datasets, or start expensive jobs. This file
is separate from the earlier Google Research takeaways.

RECOMMENDATION

Borrow the executable-answer-key idea for dokime first. Only then consider
using repeated attempts to choose practice tasks for paideia. Do not adopt the
whole EoG self-play training framework or add a knowledge graph solely to use it.
Using Metis does not require training Metis.

Metis context: dokime is the evaluation harness; paideia is the training harness;
dianoia is the reasoning layer over LTHCM. These module purposes come from the
recorded Metis architecture. No fresh repository audit was performed for this
file. Record fields, adapters and workflow steps below are proposed contracts,
not claims that the current modules expose these interfaces. Earlier reviews
reported simulated training paths in paideia; verify the current backend before
any training experiment or completion claim.

WHAT THE PAPER ESTABLISHES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

Evolve on Graph trains a shared model as a question Proposer and graph-exploring
Solver. It samples formal query structures, executes the queries on a knowledge
graph to obtain answer sets, and generates English questions from the structures.
Its curriculum rewards variation in Solver answer scores across repeated attempts.

The authors report improved knowledge-graph QA results. Those results are not
Metis results and were not independently reproduced for this review. The paper
reports 140 training steps on eight GH200 GPUs and five Solver rollouts per task.
That is their setup, not a demonstrated minimum. Local training cost, memory use,
latency and quality on Nick's hardware remain unmeasured.

Two limits must survive any adaptation:
- A complete answer set is complete for one formal query on one graph snapshot.
  It is not a claim that the graph contains all true facts about the world.
- Executing a query proves its answer set, not that the generated English asks
  the same thing. The paper uses the shared Solver with the Proposer's retrieved
  evidence as a verifier. One verification trajectory is not an independent
  semantic proof. Shared blind spots and false rejections remain possible.

ITEM 1: DOKIME EXECUTABLE MULTI-HOP EVALUATION CASES

Purpose
Build inspectable reasoning tests whose expected answers come from deterministic
execution rather than an LLM-written answer key. This should expose missed
constraints, incomplete answer sets, unsupported answers and broken evidence
chains in dianoia.

Scope
Start with a small, synthetic, read-only fact fixture and a deterministic query
executor. It may be a graph or another structured store that can express the
same joins and intersections. Do not assume LTHCM already exposes a suitable
query engine or that its retrieval results are a complete fact store. A fixture
adapter is a test dependency, not a redesign of LTHCM.

Proposed workflow
1. Freeze and hash the fixture, relation meanings and query implementation.
2. Begin with short chains and intersections. Add nested patterns only after
   the simpler executor and scoring tests pass. EoG uses six patterns; copying
   all six is not a prerequisite for this pilot.
3. Instantiate a formal query and execute it. Keep every matching answer ID,
   including alternate valid answers, rather than one sampled walk endpoint.
4. Write an English question. Keep its formal query and supporting facts.
5. Audit wording against the query before admitting the case. Check relation
   direction, scope, conjunctions, entity aliases and hidden assumptions.
   An LLM answer-set check can flag cases, but cannot be the sole authority.
   Use independently reviewed templates or a separate human-reviewed sample;
   record residual semantic uncertainty rather than calling it proven.
6. Give the system under test only the question and its permitted evidence or
   retrieval access. Keep gold answers, query traces and hidden labels out of
   the solver prompt. Any supplied anchors must be identical across test arms.
7. Save predictions, retrieved evidence, reasoning actions where available,
   and deterministic scores. Preserve failures for inspection.

Proposed case record
case_id; fixture_hash; schema_version; query_text; query_executor_version;
query_pattern; anchor_ids; question_text; gold_answer_ids; evidence_fact_ids;
wording_review_status; split; generator_version; seed.

Proposed run record
case_id; model_and_config; tested_revision; retrieval_budget; attempt_seed;
predicted_answer_ids; citations_or_evidence_ids; answer_precision;
answer_recall; answer_F1; exact_set_match; latency; token_use; failure_label.

Keep identity scoring separate from display labels so aliases do not create
false failures. Score evidence support separately from answer correctness: a
correct entity guessed without support is not evidence-grounded success.

Comparison and safeguards
- Compare model alone, a simple retrieval baseline and the Metis reasoning path
  on identical held-out cases, with comparable evidence and inference budgets.
  Report differences in access instead of calling unequal arms equivalent.
- Separate training/practice cases from evaluation cases. Hold out query
  instances, and where practical relation combinations or fixture regions.
  Do not tune the generator against the final test set.
- Add multi-answer cases, reversed relations, distractors, conflicting facts and
  missing-fact cases. For conflicts and missing facts, define expected behavior
  explicitly; a graph's closed-world answer is not automatically Metis's best
  real-world response. Report abstention separately.
- Keep a fixed evaluation set. A moving practice curriculum must not make the
  reported benchmark easier or silently replace earlier failures.

Acceptance gate
Executor unit tests must demonstrate known complete answer sets. The admitted
English cases must pass the documented wording audit, with rejected and uncertain
cases retained in the audit log. All comparison arms must use the same scoring
rules. Claim a reasoning improvement only after held-out results show it without
an unacceptable loss in evidence support, runtime or abstention behavior.
If Metis loses to simple retrieval, keep the result and fix the measured failure.

ITEM 2: PAIDEIA BOUNDARY-AWARE PRACTICE SELECTION

Purpose
Use repeated attempts to find tasks where the current model sometimes succeeds
and sometimes fails, rather than selecting only easy tasks or impossible ones.
This can begin as an offline diagnostic, without any weight updates.

Important distinction
The paper uses variation in answer F1 across five rollouts, not simply a binary
50% success target. The theoretical maximum variance on scores in [0,1] is 1/4,
attained by an equal split of zeros and ones. Five binary attempts cannot split
exactly in half. Partial answers also matter. Do not implement a rigid rule that
all useful tasks must have 50% success.

Proposed workflow
1. Take only admitted cases from the Item 1 pipeline. Bad answer keys or ambiguous
   wording must not become supposedly difficult training examples.
2. Freeze the candidate model, prompt, tool access, budgets and sampling settings.
3. Run repeated attempts with recorded seeds. Five follows the paper's setting,
   but is a noisy estimate, not a validated Metis requirement.
4. Record per-attempt answer F1, mean, variance, cost and failure labels. Separate
   reasoning variation from timeouts, tool failures and malformed outputs.
5. Propose a practice batch with some high-variation cases, plus easy retention
   checks and hard diagnostic cases. Keep the mixture explicit. All-pass and
   all-fail cases still have evaluation and diagnostic value.
6. Reassess difficulty after a genuine model or policy change. Do not infer
   learning merely because random samples improve on reused practice cases.

Why this is a proposal, not a direct port
EoG's group-relative optimization gets little answer-reward contrast when every
attempt scores alike. That observation belongs to its training setup. It does
not prove that every Metis objective, supervised update or walk-policy learner
should discard equal-score groups. Format rewards and other objectives must be
tracked separately from answer-score variance.

Training gate
Before paideia updates anything, verify that a real backend runs, parameters
actually change, a checkpoint can be loaded, and a held-out evaluation completes.
Choose the target explicitly: answer model, router or dianoia walk policy. These
are different experiments; this file does not select one or treat their rewards
as interchangeable. Any future GRPO walk-policy proposal remains separate.

Compare boundary-selected practice with uniform sampling at matched rollout and
training budgets. Report held-out answer quality, evidence support, retention,
latency and total cost. If selection adds cost without measurable benefit, keep
uniform sampling. A small local diagnostic must be measured before estimating a
larger run; no local feasibility or hardware sufficiency is established here.

ORDER OF WORK

First: fixture, executor, wording audit and fixed dokime baseline.
Second: offline repeated-attempt analysis and task-selection comparison.
Later, only with separate approval: real paideia training against a measured
failure, followed by held-out evaluation and a rollback path.

No production memory writes, self-activation, autonomous retraining, or new graph
infrastructure are implied. These are the two Metis items selected from this
paper review, not a complete Metis roadmap or a list for other projects.
