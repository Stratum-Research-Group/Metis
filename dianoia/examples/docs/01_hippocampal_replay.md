# Hippocampal Replay and Memory Consolidation

During periods of quiet wakefulness and slow-wave sleep, the mammalian hippocampus generates high-frequency field oscillations termed sharp-wave ripples (SWRs). Within these SWR events, ensembles of place cells fire in compressed temporal sequences that mirror prior behavioral trajectories.

This phenomenon, known as hippocampal replay, serves two critical cognitive functions:
1. Systems consolidation: transferring transient representations from the hippocampus to distributed neocortical circuits.
2. Deliberative trajectory planning: preplaying hypothetical routes to evaluate goals and avoid known hazards.
