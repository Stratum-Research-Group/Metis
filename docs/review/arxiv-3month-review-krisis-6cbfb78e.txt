KRISIS - TOP-PAPER TAKEAWAYS, 3 AUG - 3 NOV 2026
Two papers worth your time for Krisis. Why these: the Krisis spec (v4 line, 30 Sep) builds
selection around a claim-level support verifier with abstention. Both papers stress-test
that exact paradigm and say where it breaks. Note on currency: today's Metis repo review
(HEAD 88d9166) shows Krisis wired as an input trust boundary rather than an active
retrieval verifier, so these recommendations target the spec's direction, not current code.

====================================================================
1. Where Does Retrieval-Based Open-Ended Evaluation Fail? Automatic Taxonomy Induction from
   Long-Form Medical Answer Factuality Verification
arXiv:2609.30467 (24 Sep 2026), Huang et al. - Johns Hopkins CLSP
Coverage: PDF downloaded and read (abstract, intro, protocol, error taxonomy, results tables).

WHAT IT IS
A stress test of decompose-then-verify (FActScore/MedScore-style: split a long answer into
atomic claims, retrieve evidence, verify each claim) in open-ended medical QA (MedExpert,
clinician-annotated), plus a model-agnostic error taxonomy: retrieval-stage failures
(irrelevant evidence, scope mismatch, trustworthiness, polarity, chunking) vs
verification-stage failures (wrong evidence selection, comprehension failure, insufficient
reasoning, overconfidence, inconsistency, hallucination).

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWED (their numbers, PDF-verified)
- Best verifier recall of clinician-flagged errors on their MedExpert-gold subset (gold
  evidence guaranteed present in the corpus): 34.9% (Mistral-Small-24B); GPT-5.4 25.6%
  recall / 35.2% F1; medically fine-tuned MedGemma-27B 16.3%. Retriever ablation recalls:
  MedCPT 19.8, RRF-2 24.4, RRF-4 18.6.
- The collapse persists across model scaling, reasoning effort, medical fine-tuning, and
  corpus expansion - they argue it is structural to retrieve-then-verify in the open-ended
  regime, not stale components.
- They separate "comprehensiveness" (gold evidence may not exist in any corpus) from
  "identifiability" (it exists but is buried); identifiability improves as retrievers
  improve, comprehensiveness does not.

LIMITS
- Medical domain. The taxonomy is induced with a human-in-the-loop LLM-as-judge pipeline:
  it needs no gold answers, but that does not mean truth from nothing - human review and
  expert validation remain the ground for the induced categories, and the inducing judge is
  itself the class of system under study (they disclose this).

WHAT IT MEANS FOR KRISIS (my judgment)
- If Krisis grows the spec's claim-level support verifier, this paper maps its failure
  surface. One nuance to design for: under a binary supported/unsupported policy, "claim
  false" and "evidence missing or buried" collapse into the same outcome. Krisis's
  abstention helps only if the verifier can reliably RECOGNIZE insufficient evidence - and
  this paper's verification-stage categories (wrong evidence selection, comprehension
  failure, overconfidence) are exactly the ways that recognition fails. The taxonomy is a
  ready-made fixture list for Krisis's abstain path.
- The retrieval/verification split also bounds what any support-verifier accuracy claim can
  mean: measure and report the two stages separately, or the number conflates them.

2. The Labeling Problem in Hallucination Detection Benchmarks: An Empirical Evaluation
arXiv:2610.08026 (6 Oct 2026), Valjakka et al. - University of Helsinki
Code: https://github.com/jova486/LPHB
Coverage: PDF downloaded and read (abstract, design, main results table, prompt ablations).

WHAT IT IS
Hallucination labelers compare generated answers against reference answers, which conflates
two different criteria: reference FAITHFULNESS (every claim supported by the reference) vs
factual CORRECTNESS (no false claims). The gap between them is real claims the reference
does not happen to contain: a factually correct, well-supported-in-the-world answer can be
unfaithful to an incomplete reference, and faithfulness-strict labelers flag it anyway.
They audit automated labelers against 900 human-labeled QA pairs (3 datasets x 3 generator
models; human inter-annotator kappa 0.808, 95% CI [0.729, 0.879]).

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWED (their numbers, PDF-verified)
- Judge-vs-human kappa ranges from 0.021-0.306 (GPT-5.4, faithfulness-oriented prompt) up to
  0.693-0.740 (Claude Opus 4.7), against the 0.808 human ceiling.
- Faithfulness-approximating labelers are FALSE-POSITIVE-DOMINANT against a
  factual-correctness target - they over-flag correct answers whose true claims are absent
  from the reference.
- Switching the prompt's stated criterion from faithfulness to factual correctness improves
  agreement with humans for most judge-generator pairs; bootstrap CIs for the kappa
  difference exclude zero in all three primary comparisons. A factorial ablation isolates
  the criterion choice, not prompt elaboration, as the driver.

LIMITS
- Open-domain short-answer QA; answer-level labels, not claim-level; one human reference set.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR KRISIS (my judgment)
- Krisis's spec already separates consistency from correctness; this paper is evidence the
  separation is load-bearing. A support verifier asked "is this claim supported?" needs the
  reference scope stated: supported BY THIS EVIDENCE SET (faithfulness) and NOT FALSE
  (correctness) are different verdicts with different error shapes, and conflating them
  manufactures false positives against correct answers. The eligibility filter's abstain
  semantics should say which criterion triggers abstention.
- Their prompt-criterion methodology (same items, paired McNemar test between prompt
  variants) is the right way to A/B any Krisis policy wording change.
