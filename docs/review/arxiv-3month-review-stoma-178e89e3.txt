STOMA - TOP-PAPER TAKEAWAYS, 3 AUG - 3 NOV 2026
Why these: Stoma's documented input scope (per the project record; repo not re-inspected
for this review) is heterogeneous material - PDFs, docs, scans, photos, repos, chat
exports - ingested into LTHCM under a fail-closed, bounded-memory bar. The window gave one ingestion
ARCHITECTURE pattern worth considering, one published-weights parsing model with
low-budget-GPU ambitions, and one paper covering the camera-captured case Stoma's scope
includes.

====================================================================
1. Document Retrieval-Aware Chunking (D-RAC)
arXiv:2609.24220 (21 Sep 2026), Allu, Sivaprakash, Singh, Manocha - Yellow.ai
Coverage: PDF downloaded and read (abstract, pipeline, headline results).

WHAT IT IS / SHOWED
The authors' pipeline normalizes selected input formats (DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, scanned images,
native PDF) to PDF first, then converts rendered pages to retrieval-optimized Markdown
(tables become self-contained prose statements, heading hierarchy preserved), then chunks.
Their headline run: a 236-document, 795-page benchmark corpus processed in 72 minutes with
zero conversion or chunking errors, producing 1,748 retrieval-ready chunks; vs agentic
chunking with frontier LLMs, 95.7% fewer chunking-stage output tokens, 77.8% cost cut under
GPT-4.1 pricing (85.6% under Gemini 2.5 Pro). Caveats: the corpus is their benchmark's
size and format mix - do not generalize the zero-errors figure beyond it; cost figures are
their enterprise-API context, not your local stack.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR STOMA (my judgment)
- The normalize-to-PDF-first pattern is a candidate idea, with honest limits: PDF rendering
  is NOT faithful or deterministic for every format - spreadsheets, formulas, hidden
  content, and font-dependent layout can lose content silently, without any error for a
  fail-closed check to catch. So normalization alone does not give Stoma fail-closed
  extraction; if this pattern is considered, content preservation after rendering and
  render-time resource bounds need their own evaluation against Stoma's bar.
- The table-to-prose normalization is a specific, testable chunking choice to evaluate
  against LTHCM retrieval quality rather than assume.

2. Jina-OCR-v1: Efficient Document Parsing with Speculative Decoding and Dense Verifiable
   Rewards
arXiv:2609.03181 (2 Sep 2026), Baron Garcia et al. - Jina AI (Elastic)
Coverage: PDF downloaded and read (abstract, architecture, training).

WHAT IT IS / SHOWED
End-to-end document parsing built to serve on low-budget GPUs: DeepSeek-OCR's compressed
vision encoder + 3B MoE decoder (~570M ACTIVE params per token - decode compute, not weight
memory; the full MoE is ~3B), a FastMTP speculative decoding head sharing one draft block
recursively (K=3), greedy verification keeping decoding lossless. Post-trained with GRPO
under DENSE VERIFIABLE REWARDS - deterministic formula/table/structure checks with partial
credit. Author-reported: 91.14 on OmniDocBench v1.6 and 83.4 on olmOCRBench; 2.57 pages/s,
the highest page throughput in THEIR comparison; on an NVIDIA L4, FastMTP doubles decoding
speed over greedy AR decoding (their measurement). Weights are published on Hugging Face
(official page, verified to resolve: https://huggingface.co/jinaai/jina-ocr-v1 ).

WHAT IT MEANS FOR STOMA (my judgment)
- A candidate parsing component with published weights and published throughput numbers.
  Whether it fits and runs well on your hardware is unverified - their numbers are on an
  L4, a different GPU from yours with different capabilities; no hardware-class guarantee
  is implied. Dense-verifiable-rewards training (deterministic structural checks, partial
  credit) is also Dokime/Paideia material if a parsing-quality eval track ever appears.
- Treat throughput and benchmark scores as manufacturer-reported until reproduced.

3. TeleOCR: Navigating Document Parsing Across Digital and Camera-Captured Documents
arXiv:2608.12898 (v3, 10 Sep 2026), Cai et al. - China Telecom AI
Coverage: PDF downloaded and read (abstract, approach, headline scores).

WHAT IT IS / SHOWED
A unified parsing VLM addressing the two Stoma-relevant regimes in one model:
deformation-aware learning for camera-captured documents (geometric distortion otherwise
cascades through layout analysis) and adaptive sampling for complex high-resolution
layouts. Their overall scores: 96.87 OmniDocBench v1.6, 88.53 Wild-OmniDocBench, 78.41
PureDocBench - claimed SOTA across both end-to-end and decoupled approaches.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR STOMA (my judgment)
- The camera-captured case is in Stoma's documented ingestion scope (photos of pages,
  whiteboards); TeleOCR's deformation-aware training is the reference for why phone photos
  are a different problem from scans. For orientation only: TeleOCR's author-reported 96.87
  and Jina-OCR-v1's author-reported 91.14 are both on OmniDocBench v1.6 but come from
  separate papers with separate setups - not a controlled head-to-head - and TeleOCR's
  serving footprint is not reported in what I read (a China Telecom press release describes
  it as a 1.2B model). So: no quality-vs-footprint ranking, just two author-reported scores
  on a shared benchmark. Code/weights pages verified to resolve:
  https://github.com/caipeng328/TeleOCR and https://huggingface.co/XingChen-AGI/TeleOCR .
- Whether either model fits Stoma's streaming/fail-closed constraints is unverified.
