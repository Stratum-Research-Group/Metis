HESTIA - TOP-PAPER TAKEAWAYS, 3 AUG - 3 NOV 2026 (BATCH 2 OF THE THREE-MONTH REVIEW)
Three papers worth your time for Hestia out of the 42,755-paper pool. Why these: Hestia's
constraint is serving real models on a 16 GB card, and the window produced one frontier
KV-cache architecture report, one finding that overturns how KV eviction is done, and one
serving-speed approach that claims lossless acceleration without a draft model.

====================================================================
1. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression
arXiv:2609.19969 (17 Sep 2026), DeepSeek-AI
Attention: #1 HF daily paper of 18 Sep (180 upvotes)
Coverage: full HTML architecture sections read (CED, CSA2, hierarchical indexer, training and
deployment notes); benchmark tables skimmed, not exhaustively verified.

WHAT IT IS
A 552B-parameter multimodal MoE model built around aggressive KV-cache compression for
million-token agentic workloads. Key mechanisms:
- Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED): the decoder's global KV cache is projected from the last
  encoder layer's hidden state, so prefill computes only half the layers. 8B activated params
  per token during prefill, 16B during decode.
- Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2): three layer modes (Full / Reindex / Reuse) that share
  main KV and indexer K across layers and reuse Top-K sparse indices; plus FP4 KV caches
  during training with "marginal" degradation (their word).
- SWA Bounded Replay: approximate reconstruction of sliding-window KV states by replaying
  only the last n_win tokens instead of n_win x L/2 - a new storage-vs-compute tradeoff.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWED (their numbers)
- Global (HBM-resident) KV cache: 890 bytes per token, ~1/4 of DeepSeek-V4-Flash.
- Persistent (SSD/host) KV cache: ~1/8 of DeepSeek-V4-Flash.
- Decode FLOPs grow only ~1.25x when context extends 256x (4K to 1M).
- Claims parity with closed frontier models on most agentic benchmarks (Terminal-Bench 2.1,
  DeepSWE v1.1, AutomationBench), with a stated gap on science-heavy tasks and against giant
  closed models overall. 45T-token pretrain. Checkpoints on Hugging Face.

LIMITS
- Not runnable on your hardware: 552B backbone + 196B Engram parameters is a datacenter model.
  This is a design reference, not a deployment candidate.
- "Marginal" and "negligible" degradation claims for FP4 KV and bounded replay are theirs;
  the ablation detail exists in the paper but was not line-verified in this read.
- Post-training is deliberately standard (SFT + RL + on-policy distillation); the novelty is
  architectural and infrastructural.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR HESTIA (my judgment)
- The transferable idea is the decomposition, not the model: KV footprint = entry size x
  sequence compression x cross-layer reuse, attacked on all three axes at once. Important
  distinction: CSA2 and CED are TRAINED architectures - a model must be pretrained/finetuned
  with them; they are not runtime knobs llama.cpp can switch on for an existing GGUF. The
  runtime levers Hestia actually has (KV quantization, sliding window, prefix reuse) sit on
  the same axes, and DeepSeek's multiplicative gains are evidence about the axes' value, not
  a guarantee the same factors multiply for runtime-only composition.
- Cross-layer KV/index reuse (Full/Reindex/Reuse) is a design to watch for in future
  open-weight models and runtimes rather than adopt directly.
- SWA Bounded Replay is relevant to Hestia session persistence ONLY IF the served model
  actually uses sliding-window attention: replay reconstructs SWA KV state approximately
  from the window tokens, so on a full-attention model there is nothing to replay. It is a
  storage-vs-compute trick for SWA architectures (a trained model property), not a generic
  llama.cpp feature you can enable on any GGUF.

2. Random Attention: Rethinking KV Cache Eviction for Efficient Reasoning
arXiv:2609.03430 (3 Sep 2026), Wang, Qiu, Zhao, Qian, Yang, Han, Ji, Savarese, Heinecke,
Wang - Salesforce AI Research / UIUC
Attention: 177 upvotes, top-5 HF daily of 4 Sep
Code: https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/Random-Attention
Coverage: full HTML text read (abstract, introduction, main claims; the controlled-experiment
sections were read at claim level, not every table).

WHAT IT IS
A negative result that matters. In the paper's framing, existing KV-cache eviction methods
share one paradigm: score each token by predicted future importance, keep the top scorers.
They keep the prompt intact and evict uniformly at random within each attention head - no
scoring at all - and match the strongest scored evictor they compared against.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWED (their numbers)
- Across four models and six reasoning tasks at ~4x compression, random eviction matches the
  strongest prior evictor on average accuracy.
- With no scoring pass, it serves 32-43% higher throughput than the best scorer (TriAttention)
  in vLLM at 32k-token generations.
- The mechanism, from their controlled experiments: (1) the prompt is the fragile part of the
  cache - most of the gap between selectors is whether they happened to keep it; (2) the
  reasoning trace protects itself through redundancy - the model restates what it still needs
  in the text, and each attention head keeps its own copy - so once the prompt is safe, a
  random draw retains enough.

LIMITS
- Applies to long chain-of-thought reasoning workloads; small remaining gaps to the best
  scorer on code reasoning with long prompts (budget consumed by the prompt).
- "Matches on average across six tasks" is not "matches on every task"; per-task variance
  exists in their tables.
- This is an eviction result, not a compression-format result; it says nothing about
  quantization or low-rank KV approaches.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR HESTIA (my judgment)
- Directly actionable as a baseline: if Hestia ever implements KV eviction for long
  reasoning traces, "keep prompt, evict randomly per head" is simple in principle and, in
  their vLLM deployment, serves 32-43% higher throughput than the best scorer. Any smarter
  eviction you add must beat random - their results say that is harder than the literature
  implies. Actually wiring eviction into llama.cpp/Hestia is untested engineering, not a
  config flag.
- Their mechanistic finding argues the prompt is the fragile part of the cache and the
  reasoning trace protects itself through redundancy - so cache policy should protect the
  prompt/system/context prefix first and can be much more relaxed about the generated trace.
- Also a Dokime-relevant evaluation point: eviction quality claims need throughput and
  per-task breakdowns, not just average accuracy.

3. Unlocking Lossless Speedups in LLMs via Discrete Diffusion ("Uno")
arXiv:2609.04010 (3 Sep 2026), Sahoo et al.
Attention: 150 upvotes, #1 HF daily paper of 8 Sep
Coverage: PDF downloaded and read (method and contribution sections; experiment tables read
at claim level).

WHAT IT IS (PDF-verified)
Diffusion-augmented autoregressive models ("Uno"): the frozen AR weights define the target
distribution and verify candidates; a lightweight LoRA-parameterized diffusion pathway
proposes multiple tokens in parallel. Because the frozen AR model is the verifier, the
sampling distribution is preserved - hence "lossless", in the same sense as speculative
decoding. Two variants: trained from scratch on proprietary data, and an augmentation of the
open-weight Qwen3-8B.
WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWED (their numbers, with the settings attached)
- Throughput test setup (their Sec. 5): 1,024 random input tokens + fixed 8,192 output
  tokens ("1K/8K throughput test"); measured at batch size 1 and at the largest batch size
  that fits on ONE H200 GPU; tokens-per-forward-pass equalized across methods so all are
  compared at the same input and effective output lengths.
- Up to 3x speedup over the base AR model (abstract); their own contribution statement is
  more careful: up to 2x at the LARGEST supported batch size - the regime where prior
  diffusion-LLM speedups vanish. These are their measurements on their setup, not a
  universal win claim.
- The 8B Uno outperforms the 26B DiffusionGemma and the proprietary Mercury 2 across their
  evaluated benchmarks (agentic tool use, coding, long-context reasoning).
- Higher throughput than leading speculative-decoding methods at every evaluated batch size
  in their 1K/8K test.
- Code and checkpoints released.

LIMITS
- "Lossless" means distribution-preserving verification against the frozen AR model, as with
  speculative decoding - not that quality claims are independently replicated.
- Requires training diffusion weights on top of a base model - not a drop-in for an existing
  GGUF in llama.cpp. The 8B augmentation recipe exists; whether it lands in llama.cpp-style
  runtimes is an open engineering question.
- Benchmark wins are against the d-LLM field (DiffusionGemma, Mercury 2), not against the
  best AR models at equal size.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR HESTIA (my judgment)
- If the claims hold, this is a third serving-acceleration lane between speculative decoding
  (which Hestia could already use with a draft model) and pure diffusion LLMs: speed without a
  second model in memory - attractive on a 16 GB card where a draft model competes with the
  main model for VRAM.
Note: this entry is based on a full PDF read (method, experiments setup, throughput
analysis); the per-benchmark quality tables were read at claim level, not line-verified.
